Practice test

The real test has 60 questions with 3 choices each, 2 hours, and you need 70% (42 right). Aim for 85% or more here before Monday.

Chart lab

Why this matters: since October 26, 2026 the UAG test shows images of current sectional charts on screen, in addition to the printed testing supplement. Expect a handful of chart questions on airspace, airport data, frequencies and obstacles.

1. Practice map

    2. Real chart drill: your own backyard

    Open the real San Francisco sectional on SkyVector at Palo Alto (PAO), zoom in, and answer:

    1. What color is the Palo Alto airport symbol, and what does that tell you?Blue: it has a control tower, so it sits in Class D (dashed blue). Read its Class D ceiling in the box; look for a minus sign, since SFO's Class B sits above.
    2. Find SFO. What line style surrounds it, and what does "100/SFC" on the inner ring mean?Solid blue = Class B. 100/SFC = surface up to 10,000 ft MSL. Each outer ring has its own floor, e.g. 30 = 3,000 ft MSL.
    3. Find San Jose (SJC). What class is it?Solid magenta rings = Class C. Read the inner (xx/SFC) and outer (xx/12 style) labels.
    4. Find Moffett (NUQ). Can you fly a drone at the Stanford campus edge near it without authorization?Moffett is a blue, towered airport in Class D. If your spot is inside the dashed blue line (or under Class B/C floors that reach the surface), you need LAANC authorization. Check the LAANC grid in a LAANC app for allowed altitudes.
    5. Find Half Moon Bay (HAF) on the coast. Towered or not? Where is its CTAF?Magenta symbol = non-towered. CTAF is the frequency followed by Ⓒ in its data block.
    6. Pick any obstacle near the bay. Find its MSL and AGL heights and work out the ground elevation.Bold number = MSL top; number in parentheses = AGL. Ground = MSL − AGL.

    Three day plan

    The test leans heavily on Regulations, Operations and Airspace. One source citing the PSI bulletin gives the split as Regulations 48%, Operations 25%, Airspace 20%, Weather 5%, Loading 2%. Older guides show a more even split, so cover everything but put your time where the points are.

    1. Fri Nov 6 (tonight, 45 min): Flashcards: Regulations. Read the Cheat sheet once.
    2. Sat Nov 7 (2 to 3 hrs): Flashcards: Airspace & Charts and Operations. Do the Chart lab practice map. Quick 15 test twice. Read the Diagrams tab.
    3. Sun Nov 8 (2 to 3 hrs): Flashcards: Weather and Loading. Real chart drill on SkyVector. Full 60 question mock under the timer. Then "Redo my misses". Go through the 40 FAA sample questions with figures. Last chance to cancel or reschedule for a refund: 7:30 PM Sunday.
    4. Mon Nov 9 (day of): Morning: one more full mock. Afternoon: only "cards I missed" and the Cheat sheet. Stop studying by 5:30 PM, eat, hydrate.

    Ready signal: 85% or more on two full mocks.

    Test day checklist (Mon Nov 9)

    During the test

    • About 2 minutes per question; you'll have extra time. Flag hard ones and come back.
    • Read every word. "MSL" vs "AGL" and "true" vs "magnetic" are the classic traps.
    • For chart questions: find the point, identify the line color and style, then read the numbers. Subtract ground elevation when converting MSL to AGL.
    • Three choices: if unsure, eliminate the one that is clearly wrong, then pick the safer or more regulation-based answer.

    After you pass

    1. Keep your Airman Knowledge Test Report (AKTR). It has your 17-digit Knowledge Test Exam ID.
    2. Log into IACRA, start an application for Pilot → Remote Pilot (Form 8710‑13), enter the Exam ID, sign and submit.
    3. TSA does a security check. Then you get an email to print a temporary certificate; the plastic card comes by mail.
    4. Register your drone under Part 107 on FAADroneZone and put the number on the outside.
    5. Set a reminder for recurrent training in 24 calendar months.

    If you don't pass: the report lists missed ACS codes. You can retake after a 14 day wait, bringing the failed AKTR as your retest authorization.
